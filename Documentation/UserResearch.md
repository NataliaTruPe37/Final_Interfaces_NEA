We did a previous general investigation before doing individual interviews of 10 minutes.

According to an ENSAFI article, at least 36.2% of the Mexican population that are 18 or more years old reported having some kind of debt; El Imparcial (2026) cites Condusef in reporting that 70% of young people conduct all their financial transactions through banking apps and that this accelerates their accumulation of debt.

Although it is undeniable that the rise in this type of apps is one of the reasons so many young people are falling into debt without being in such a dire financial situation, it can't be denied that there is a clear lack of financial literacy. According to ENIF, just 6% of adults had taken a course on saving, budgeting, or responsible use of credit, and 23% kept a budget.

Interviews:

Participant 01:

General commentaries based on what he talked to us: He is a foreigner, he receives money from his parents once a week for his groceries. Their parents pay his rent and collegiature directly.

He constantly buys snacks and things like cookies, donuts and snacks in the cafeteria of his university. This doesn’t leave him without money but makes up for some parts of the groceries.

On more than one occasion, he didn't buy some groceries because he didn't have enough money.

He mentions that once, during the last two days before receiving money from her parents for groceries, she didn't eat dinner and rationed her food quite a bit, because otherwise it wouldn't have lasted.

Influence on the application: The categories of expenses, where we ask the user to cover their basic expenses before making any other purchases.

Participant 02:

General commentaries based on what he talked to us: He didn't give us much information; he says he hasn't talked much about how much he spends each week on food and going out.

Although he's a scholarship student, that money naturally goes toward tuition; the money he has which he uses to buy things at the cafeteria and when he goes out with his friends comes from his parents.

Influence on the application: The quantity of the money visible in the shopping part and the feedback telling you how much you spend, warning you if you save poorly.

START: The user opens the app to manage his/her expenses.

Phase 1: The user defines the payment he gets and how often does he/she receive it

Phase 2: The user writes his most important expenses for the week, biweek or month

Phase 3: The user can add other expenses, equally important or not that important as their basic ones in phase 3 to identify everything he/she’ll need to buy/pay

Phase 4: As he/she makes his/her payment, he fills the indicators of the product/payment

Phase 5: He/she pays the basic stuff needed and some (or maybe all) of the not so important payments and clicks the “Finish” button

END: The user receives the indicator of how much money he/she save

FRICTION: An indicator will pop out if the student spends all his money and will not let him continue

OPPORTUNITY: The app will invite him to check his payments and verify if they are correct
